# Story log: Jordan (fictional sample)

**Date:** March 2026
**Situation:** A platform migration sat two weeks from its deadline, and three dependent teams disagreed on what "done" meant.
**Action:** Facilitated a one-hour working session, drafted a shared exit checklist, and obtained sign-off from each team lead.
**Result:** Migration shipped on schedule with zero rollback events. The checklist became the template for later migrations.
**Lesson:** Agree on the definition of done at kickoff. Disputes found late cost far more than disputes found early.
**Skills and themes:** Stakeholder alignment, scope definition, risk management
**Growth area:** Raising scope disagreements earlier

---

**Date:** November 2025
**Situation:** An SOC 2 evidence deadline was six weeks out and 14 controls had no owner after a reorg.
**Action:** Mapped each control to the team that now owned the system, ran a 20-minute weekly review with the security lead, and escalated the two controls nobody would take to the director.
**Result:** All 14 controls had evidence two weeks before the audit window. One control was redesigned instead of patched.
**Lesson:** Ownership gaps after a reorg are invisible until something needs an owner. Check them on day one.
**Skills and themes:** Compliance programs, escalation, cross-team ownership
**Growth area:** Escalating sooner instead of absorbing the work myself

---

**Date:** June 2025
**Situation:** A mobile launch I ran slipped four weeks because a payments SDK upgrade was discovered late.
**Action:** Ran the retro, traced the miss to a dependency that lived only in one engineer's head, and added a dependency review to the kickoff checklist.
**Result:** The launch shipped four weeks late. The next two launches had no late dependency surprises.
**Lesson:** If a dependency is not written down, it does not exist for planning purposes.
**Skills and themes:** Dependency management, retrospectives, launch readiness
**Growth area:** Asking "what lives only in someone's head?" before committing a date
