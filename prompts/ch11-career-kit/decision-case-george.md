# George's decision (the book's case study)

George is a fictional senior TPM weighing his current role against an external offer. Paste this with P11.18 to see how the three frameworks pull apart on the same facts.

- The current role pays $150,000 base, with annual equity refreshers and a promotion review in about 18 months that his manager says will be likely.
- The offer pays $180,000 base, plus a four-year RSU grant that vests quarterly. The company is public and the vesting schedule becomes a semi-predictable windfall.
- The offer's scope is leading a platform program George has wanted to run for years. The current role scope is steady maintenance of existing programs.
- The offer includes an on-call rotation requirement of one week in every six. The current role has none.
- George is his household's only income.
- The family holds three months of expenses in savings.
- The offer is fully remote. The current role requires three office days a week.

**What the book found (Table 11.1):**

| Framework | Fact it weighs most | Where it leans |
|---|---|---|
| Regret minimization | The program scope and its growth orientation over stagnation | Toward the offer |
| Value maximization | The RSU terms against the promotion path | Undecided until the RSU terms are known |
| Stability weighting | Sole income, thin savings, and the reorganization | Toward staying |

The facts never change, yet each framework can lead to a different outcome. Deciding which factors matter most is yours to do.
