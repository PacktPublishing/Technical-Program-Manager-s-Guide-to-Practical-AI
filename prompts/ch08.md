# Chapter 8 - AI for TPM Leadership: every prompt

Every skill, agent and prompt in the chapter has a working file in the [Chapter 8 kit](ch08-tpm-leadership/README.md), plus synthetic test inputs and the output it produced. The numbers below (8.01 to 8.26) match the kit's file numbers.

**Two ways to use it:**

1. **Any AI tool:** open a prompt file, fill in the `[placeholders]`, paste it, then paste the "Try it with" files underneath.
2. **Claude Code:** open the kit folder (`cd prompts/ch08-tpm-leadership` then `claude`). Its `CLAUDE.md` registers `/program-3p-report`, `/portfolio-executive-report` and the `portfolio-reporting-agent`. In Claude.ai, zip each skill folder and upload it under Customize > Skills.

> **People data comes first.** Resumes, interview feedback, 1:1 notes and promotion packets are personal data. New York City requires bias audits for automated hiring tools, and the EU AI Act classifies recruitment AI as high-risk. Sometimes anonymized resumes are enough; sometimes candidates must consent. When in doubt, ask Legal or HR, and check your company's AI usage policy. Label people "Candidate A", "Candidate B" or "TPM-R" before you upload anything, and keep generated output private. All test data in the kit is synthetic and already anonymized.

The prompt files are the tested versions. They contain `[placeholders]`: the "Try it with" column gives the values used in the test run (full list in [RUNS.md](ch08-tpm-leadership/tests/chapter-8/RUNS.md)), so you can reproduce each sample output. Where testing showed a printed prompt needed a fix (for example "ask three questions **and wait for my answers**"), the file carries the fix; [CHANGES.md](ch08-tpm-leadership/docs/chapter-8/CHANGES.md) lists each change and why.

**8.20 in Socratic mode** (the book's alternative for practising sensitive news). The AI asks one question at a time and never answers for you:

```
I need to talk with [person] about [situation]. Act in Socratic mode: ask me one question at a time to help me prepare. Do not give advice or write the script.
```

The book's example: *"I need to talk with Alex about errors in the last two status reports that have cost stakeholder trust..."* The questions move from the problem to the outcome ("What would a successful outcome look like?"), from assumption to curiosity ("What do you actually know about why the errors happened, and what are you assuming?"), and from the conversation to what happens afterward ("How will you know, a month from now, that trust is coming back?").
